Your patients are waiting.

Take the history, check the vitals, order only what you need, and make the call. Each case is one assignment. Keep your patients alive and your medical license in good standing.

EMERGENCY · WAITING ROOM

Sample cases written for teaching, awaiting review by a clinician. Educational simulation, not medical advice. Patients are fictional.

For authors: the case data format

Each case is one record in the CASES list. Every action has a kind: key (expected; skipping it costs points), ok (reasonable, no cost), extra (unnecessary: costs points and patient condition) or harm (hurts the patient). Each answer option can carry an info card. Each patient's illustration is set in LOOKS.


    

  






  
  

    
How to play SORE!

    
You're the doctor on shift. Each case is a patient with a problem to solve. Your job is to find out what's wrong without wasting time or hurting anyone.

    
1. Read the briefing

    
Each unit has a briefing with the key facts, like normal vital sign ranges. Read it first; the cases expect you to know it.

    
2. Meet the patient

    
Read the patient's story on the chart. Think: what could cause this? What do I need to find out?

    
3. Investigate

    
    
      
  1. Click any history question, vital sign, exam or test to review it first: what it checks and any risks.
    2. 
      
  2. Press Order this to do it, or Cancel to back out. Reviewing is free; ordering is not.
    3. 
      
  3. Results are written onto the patient's chart.
    4. 
    

    
Watch the patient's condition bar, and their face. Unnecessary orders waste time and drop the bar by 10. Harmful orders injure your patient and drop it by 30. Below 50, the case fails even with the right diagnosis.

    
4. Decide

    
You'll answer two questions, usually which structure or vital sign is the problem and then the diagnosis. Click an answer to read about it before you commit, then press Next or Submit diagnosis.

    
5. Face the attending

    
Dr. Okafor, the attending physician, reviews every case. You'll get your score, the debrief, and her judgement.

Your medical license

    
    
      
  • Full recovery: right diagnosis, right step 2 answer, no harm, and careful testing. Removes one strike.
    • 
      
  • Recovered, with complaints: the patient lives, but your care was sloppy. No strike.
    • 
      
  • Lawsuit: wrong diagnosis, or you harmed the patient badly. One strike.
    • 
      
  • Three strikes and your license is suspended. Come back to this page to be reinstated.
    • 
    

    
6. Turn it in

    
After each case, enter your name and press Download PDF report. Upload the PDF to the matching assignment in Google Classroom (Add or create → File) and press Turn in. If the download doesn't work on your device, press Copy my results and paste them into the assignment instead.

    
Tips from the attending

    
    
      
  • Start with the history and a full set of vitals. They solve more cases than any scanner.
    • 
      
  • Always ask about medications and allergies.
    • 
      
  • Never force a possibly broken bone to move, or make an injured patient walk "to see."
    • 
      
  • Before every order, ask: will this result change what I do?
