For authors: the case data format

Each case is one record in the CASES list. Every action has a kind: key (expected; skipping it costs points), ok (reasonable, no cost), extra (unnecessary: costs points and patient condition) or harm (hurts the patient). Each answer option can carry an info card. Each patient's illustration is set in LOOKS.

How to play ↓

    

    

  


  

    
Your shift, in four moves

    
Every case is a real-feeling patient. Your job is to find out what's wrong without wasting time or hurting anyone.

    

      
Triage
Twelve patients arrive at once. Read each triage card (what they say, their vitals, what you see) and rank them from 1 Immediate to 4 Non-urgent.

      
Investigate
Click any question, vital sign, exam or test to review it first: what it checks, its risks, and how long it takes. Then Order this or Cancel. Reviewing is free; ordering isn't.

      
Decide
Answer two questions: which structure or vital sign is the problem, then the diagnosis. Click an answer to read about it before you commit.

      
Face the attending
Dr. Aprukuma reviews every patient. Get a full recovery, a patient who recovers with complaints, or a lawsuit.

    

  


  

    

      
The clock is always running

      
In a shift, every question, exam and test moves the clock forward for everyone still waiting. Reading and thinking are free. Each triage level has a time limit. Patients who wait longer get worse, start their visit in poorer condition, and cost you points. Watch their faces on the board.

Crisis ER: Full Moon Shift mixes 12 random patients from every system you've studied. Expect surprise ambulances mid-shift (not every ambulance is an emergency) and one shared CT scanner: if it's busy, you wait. Your teacher can give the whole class a shift code so everyone gets the same patients.

      
Real emergency departments use scales like the Emergency Severity Index (ESI). The game's levels and times are simplified and run on game minutes.

Condition bar: an unnecessary order drops it 10, and a harmful one drops it 30. Below 50, the patient's case fails, even with the right diagnosis.

Tips from Dr. Aprukuma

      
    
        
  • Start with the history and a full set of vitals. They solve more cases than any scanner.
    • 
        
  • Always ask about medications and allergies.
    • 
        
  • Never force a possibly broken bone to move, or make an injured patient walk "to see."
    • 
        
  • Before every order, ask: will this result change what I do?
    • 
        
  • Read the unit briefing first. The cases expect you to know it.
